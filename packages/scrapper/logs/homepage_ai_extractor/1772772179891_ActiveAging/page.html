About Us

We aim to drive a
paradigm shift in the
relationship between
US health plans and
their aging members.

An increase in life expectancy and the growing prevalence of chronic conditions
is causing an explosion in healthcare expenditure. Health plans are at a
crossroads, needing more control to better understand, manage and engage
with each member’s health journey to improve outcomes and reduce this
financial burden.  At ActiveAging we are driving this transformation.

 

Founded in 2020, ActiveAging is led by a highly experienced and visionary team
of entrepreneurs. The company is uniquely targeting, Medicare Advantage health
plans, the fastest-growing health insurance segment with a 10% CAGR between
2019–2023, reaching 31.4M members and 50% penetration rate.

US Health Plans: FAQs

Medicare Advantage health plans or health payers is a large
complex ecosystem of many players and unique terminology.

Here we try to answer some of your most frequently asked
questions when learning about ActiveAging.

What is a health plan?

A health plan is a type of health insurance that you buy in order to pay for the cost of medical treatment if you are ill or injured. There are two types of health insurances in the US, private and public. Most people use a combination of both. The US public health insurances are: Medicare, Medicaid, and Children’s Health Insurance Program.

What is Original Medicare and Medicare Advantage?

Original Medicare is the US federal health insurance program for people aged 65 and older, as well as some younger people with disabilities. It is a fee-for-service health plan that has two parts: Part A (Hospital Insurance) and Part B (Medical Insurance).

Medicare Advantage (MA, also known as “Part C”) is a type of Medicare health plan offered by a private company that contracts with Medicare. These plans offer Part A, Part B, and usually Part D (Drug Coverage). MA Plans may offer some extra benefits that Original Medicare doesn’t cover. ActiveAging is specifically targeting the fast-growing MA health plan which has about 50% of US Medicare beneficiaries enrolled.

How do health plans get paid?

MA health plans are provided by Medicare-approved private companies who receive monthly capitated payments from the CMS (Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services) to cover their members’ health expenses (physicians, hospitals, etc.). These payments are calculated annually based on a risk adjustment score, derived from a combination of demographics and diagnosed chronic conditions, which estimates each member next year’s annual health expense.  Once set, this score and payments are fixed for the rest of the year. As a result if a members health deteriorates, this may lead to substantial losses for the health plan.

In Memory of Major Tal Grushka‏

Major Tal Grushka, a company commander in the 931st Battalion of the Nahal Brigade IDF, was killed on October 7th while defending the residents of the Gaza envelope.

He was born and raised in Kfar Saba, where he attended Ben Gurion School and Katznelson High School, majoring in theater. He was a virtuoso pianist, an outstanding athlete, a master in conveying messages both in writing and verbally, a charismatic leader attentive to his subordinates, and possessed exceptional social sensitivity, familial and social dedication.

In continuation of his work and as part of his will, a project has been initiated encouraging soldiers and children to call their grandparents to wish them a peaceful Shabbat.

He was 26 at the time of his death – living in our hearts.
