Normally open(NO) and Normally closed (NC) are terms used to define the states that switches, sensors or relay contacts are under when they are not activated.

A NO contact or a normally open contact is the one that remains open until a certain condition is satisfied such as a button being pressed or some other manner of activation such as those based on temperature, pressure, etc.

A NC contact or normally closed contact is the exact opposite of NO contact by function. It remains closed until a certain condition is satisfied.

Lighting control cabinets typically control a group of street lights or advertising signage from a “control cabinet”. These controls have historically provided on-off functionality based on the time of day using an “astronomical clock”-based switch or daylight photosensor. Lights are controlled in groups with no individual control over a specific light.

Although new controllers such as Tondo’s Edge-IQ controller have replaced the cabinet-based approach with new technologies that include advanced dimming, remote cloud-control, and support for functionality including sensors and switches, there are many outdoor lights and signs that do not support on-lamp control. CAT-M/LTE-M and NB-IoT are similar but have differences that may make one suitable over another, or simply selected based on the support for one or the other that is available in your area.

NB-IoT uses a narrow bandwidth of 200 kHz, where CAT-M uses 1.4 MHz. The maximum data rate for NB-IoT is ~ 250 kb per second, with CAT-M1 reaching ~ 1 Mbps. CAT-M is marginally less energy efficient than NB-IoT. Although NB-IoT has a lower speed, both NB-IoT and CAT-M are suitable for sensor communications since sensors typically do not require much bandwidth.

Both NB-IoT and CAT-M1 are supported under the 5G technology specifications and therefore are ideal for selecting as a standard for sensor communications.

 

CAT-M wireless (aka LTE-M) is a low-power wide area network (LPWAN) cellular data transmission standard that operates over the data and physical layer. CAT-M was designed for IoT projects, with an average upload speed between 200 kbps and 400 kbps.

Eddystone is an open-source Bluetooth advertising protocol originally designed by Google. It can be used by mobile device applications to deliver improved proximity-based experiences that include applications such as Google Maps.

These packets can be discovered with any Bluetooth LE APIs such as Core Bluetooth on iOS, or android.bluetooth.le on Android. You can also use them with Google’s Nearby Messages API, which can be integrated into an iOS or Android app, and receive “messages” in those apps when a person enters or exits a range of beacons.

You can read more about it on github.com/google/eddystone.

The NEMA ANSI C137.4-2021 builds on the NEMA C137.41 7-pin connector standard and the DALI communication protocol. It has additional characteristics and features that align very closely with the D4i family of specifications from the DALI Alliance.

D4i and ANSI C137.4-2021 specify the digital communication between luminaires and devices including sensors and network lighting controllers. The expanded ANSI C137.4-2021 now includes energy reporting data and diagnostics and maintenance data.

The NEMA ANSI C137.10 standard specifies roadway and area lighting equipment connector compatibility. The 3-pin standard does not provide for dimming control, but provides for on/off operation. The later standard C137.41 adds dimming control (5- and 7-pin connectors) and sensor control (7-pin connectors). The newer C137.4-2021 standard provides enhanced functionality and compatibility with the DALI D4i lighting and sensor control standard.

The NEMA ANSI C137.41 standard specifies covers roadway and area lighting equipment connection interoperability. The 7-pin receptacle provides for dimming control and sensor communications.

The NEMA ANSI C137.41 5-pin connector variant adds support for dimming control, but does not include sensor communications support which is supported by the 7-pin connector.

DALI, or Digital Addressable Lighting Interface, is a dedicated protocol for digital lighting control that enables the easy installation of robust, scalable and flexible lighting networks.

Wiring is relatively simple; DALI power and data is carried by the same pair of wires, without the need for a separate bus cable.

Edge IQ: ADVANCED LIGHTING CONTROL

Street lighting and power are the keys to the night-time economy. Managed intelligently, they make a city run safely, efficiently and cost-effectively.

Tondo’s Edge IQ controller is available for socketed NEMA or Zhaga fixtures, internal decorative fixtures, internal socket replacement installation, or internal pole installation. Edge IQ delivers data-driven remote control and monitoring—from the pole to the control room—with precise sunrise/sunset operation, adaptive response to traffic and conditions, and early fault detection. Field crews receive smart prioritization and guided dispatch/navigation by urgency, improving maintenance accuracy and budgets. 

Cabinet IQ: Energy Service Analytics

Tondo’s Cabinet IQ Energy Analytics system can alert cities to problems with the quality of upstream utility service to their streetlights, and when used with Edge IQ fixture-based controls, Cabinet IQ can diagnose downstream circuit anomalies.

This allows cities to identify problems before trucks are dispatched, reducing unproductive truck rolls that can reduce maintenance costs by 50%-75%. 

Cloud IQ AI Powered Management

Tondo’s Cloud-IQ Central Management System (CMS) is an AI-powered cloud management platform that delivers safer, standards-based lighting, optimizes operations and maintenance, reduces energy use, transforms data into actionable insights, and unifies all IoT devices into a single, secure network.

Cloud-IQ provides robust reporting on Edge IQ, Cabinet IQ, and sensor-collected data, energy usage and quality, and alerts operations teams to system anomalies. Cloud-IQ connects easily with asset and maintenance management systems, CRM, ERP, third-party analytics, lighting outage web pages and  mobile community apps.

Protecting Urban Electrical Infrastructure from Theft and Tampering

Urban electrical infrastructure faces rising threats: electricity theft from unauthorized connections, the theft of copper cables, and copper theft from on-pole transformers. The impact is immediate – dark streets, failed signals, and service disruptions that erodes safety, quality of life, and public trust.

Tondo connects directly to the utility service point, applies real-time AI anomaly detection, and cross-references multiple data sources to alert teams before incidents become outages.

Streetlight Copper Wire Theft

Available January 2026 Streetlight wire theft has become an epidemic as copper prices continue to rise, with easy accessibility for thieves at the ground level.

Tondo’s new Wire Theft IQ sensors are installed at the utility service point and end of each circuit, providing line continuity & load/impedance monitoring on powered or unpowered circuits, with optional access panel, vibration and tilt sensing. Real time alerts are sent to operations teams with estimated location, with near 100% accuracy. This allows cities and utilities to work with their law enforcement partners to make copper wire theft risky and unprofitable.

Electricity Theft

Available Now Utilities have reported line loss from electricity theft at 1% – 3% of total generation. Although residential and commercial building smart meters have helped, line loss from streetlight infrastructure largely remains unknown.

Tondo’s Cabinet IQ and Cloud IQ AI detect electricity theft events in real-time. Cabinet IQ identifies illegal taps/bypasses (load, phase balance, abnormal current and voltage). Real-time alerts with location and priority are sent to operations teams for immediate action.

Pole Transformer Theft

Available NowUtility pole transformers can contain 125 lbs/55 kg of copper each and more depending on their size. With copper market pricing over $5.00/lb USD, transformers have become a new target for copper thieves.

Transformer theft is detected with Tondo’s Cabinet IQ + Cloud IQ AI: abnormal electrical signatures, door/cover sensor events, and sudden load shifts create unique digital signatures that identify theft events with high accuracy. Real-time alerts are sent to operations teams and provide guided dispatch to thefts-in-progress.

Protecting Urban Airspace

The sky infrastructure is a critical – and largely unmanaged – layer of the city.

Urban low-altitude airspace is becoming crowded with drones and small aircraft that can threaten critical infrastructure and sensitive areas. Tondo’s track record of innovation brings a new solution to solve this problem before it is too late: Drone IQ.

Registered UAV/Drone Detection

Available Q2 2026 With the low cost and ability for anyone to purchase a drone, owners of critical infrastructure must contend with new threats. Tondo’s new Drone IQ detection system provides a new affordable solution for critical infrastructure owners.

The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) and Transport Canada require all drones over 0.55 lbs/250 grams, or any drones of any size used for commercial purposes to be registered. Both countries restrict airspace around national security areas, airports, flight paths, and areas designated as temporary flight restricted areas. However, these areas do not protect many other areas with critical public infrastructure from airborne threats. 

Tondo’s UAV/Drone IQ drone detection system captures the Drone ID of any drone within range of the area protected by Drone IQ, logs the ID, location, altitude, velocity, direction, and the location of the drone operator. Drones entering sensitive areas – whether FAA/Transport Canada restricted or not – are alerted to potential threats to critical infrastructure so that they can notify their law enforcement partners to take immediate action.

UNREGISTERED UAV/DRONE DETECTION

Available Q2 2026 Current drone detection systems are extremely expensive and large, impractical for protecting dense urban areas. Tondo has developed an accurate, affordable new UAV/drone detection solution designed to deploy on streetlight fixtures as well as off-pole applications.

For UAVs/drones that is not required to support Drone ID, or where an operator has failed to register or intentionally disabled Drone ID, Tondo’s Drone IQ uses acoustic sensors and artificial intelligence to detect the unique audio signature of a drone.

Drone IQ captures the location, direction and speed of the drone, and sends an alert to the Tondo CMS when a drone enters a protected area – whether or not that area is FAA/Transport Canada restricted or not.

Once alerted, security teams can alert law enforcement partners to take appropriate action – fast.
