Recent data on Denosumab in advanced CKD and dialysis patients highlights a critical gap in bone therapeutics.

Higher rates of major cardiovascular events and hypocalcemia, without a clear fracture benefit over bisphosphonates, raise important questions in one of the most fragile patient populations.

This is not a single-drug issue.
It reflects how bone therapies have long been developed in isolation.

For patients where bone, kidney, cardiovascular health, and mineral metabolism are tightly linked, efficacy alone is not enough.
Safety across interconnected systems must be part of the design.

At OrthoTreat, this is the challenge we’re addressing: working with the body to restore its optimal state for bone regeneration.

Finally, some genuinely good news for pharma, for bones, and for aching people everywhere. The FDA just made history, qualifying bone mineral density (BMD) as a validated surrogate endpoint for osteoporosis drug development.
This is the first surrogate endpoint qualified under the 21st Century Cures Act, and it’s a big deal.

What does this change?
→ Trial timelines cut from ~5 years to ~2 years
→ Patient enrollment reduced by ~10×
→ Development costs slashed by ~10×

After no new osteoporosis therapies since 2019, the field was stagnant. Now the floodgates are open.
Major props to Entera Bio and all the companies working to advance osteoporosis treatment, this levels the playing field and accelerates innovation when millions of patients need it most.

Tea or coffee?
Your bones might have an opinion.
A long-term observational study following older women found something interesting: regular tea consumption was associated with slightly higher bone mineral density, while very high coffee intake showed the opposite trend in certain contexts. No villains here. No miracle drinks either. The effects are modest, but the message is bigger.
Bone isn’t static. It’s living tissue, constantly responding to biology, habits, and environment. Small, everyday choices can quietly influence long-term skeletal health. Just like what we’re learning about nutrition and ultra-processed foods, bone health isn’t about one dramatic intervention.
It’s about biology over time.

Based on recent research published in Nutrients (MDPI), alongside broader public health discussions on lifestyle and bone health (including coverage in Ynet).
