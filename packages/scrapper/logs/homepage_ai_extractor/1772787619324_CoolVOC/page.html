Our Technology

VOC Capture/Adsorption

Exhaust from a tool flows through a zeolite bed, where pollutants (VOCs) are adsorbed until saturation. Clean air is then released. The length of this process and the frequency of regeneration depends on VOC concentration; low concentrations mean prolonged zeolite use, making it cost-effective.

Zeolite Bed Regeneration

Once the zeolite bed reaches its saturation point with adsorbed VOCs, a carefully orchestrated regeneration process is initiated. This entails the systematic release of the accumulated VOCs in a concentrated form, allowing the zeolite bed to return to its optimal state for subsequent air purification cycles.

Cooling / Condensation

The concentrated VOC stream undergoes a patented condensation process, transforming it into a liquid solvent. Subsequently, the condensed VOCs are extracted from the system and redirected for additional utilization.

​Advantages of VOC Recycling

It's cool to recycle, this is why:

Climate Change Mitigation

​Incineration is a common method to terminate VOCs from the industrial exhaust. The burning of waste contributes to climate change by releasing CO2 gases into the atmosphere. We can reduce climate change impacts by recycling VOCs, thereby reducing their emissions and promoting sustainable practices.

Health Benifits

Various health issues can be caused by VOCs, including respiratory issues, headaches, dizziness, and irritation to the eyes and throat. Recycling VOCs minimizes their presence in the environment and mitigates the health risks they pose.

Resource Conservation

Fossil fuels and non-renewable resources are the primary sources of VOCs. By recycling VOCs, we are able to recover valuable compounds and reuse them, eliminating the need to extract and produce new raw materials. This conservation of resources minimizes energy consumption and decreases the environmental footprint associated with their production.
