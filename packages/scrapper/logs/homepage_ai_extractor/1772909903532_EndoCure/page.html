Ultrasound imaging today

Ultrasound remains a highly utilized imaging modality due to its safety, affordability, and real-time capabilities. However, traditional ultrasound is prone to operator dependency, significant inter and intra observer variability, and physical strain on sonographers.

Global ultrasound market: $9.5B (2024); Pelvic imaging and gynecology ultrasound alone is projected to exceed $1.3B by 2028. Robotic ultrasound expected CAGR >16%.

Tech Falls Short

Despite technological advances, ultrasound and MRI often produce incomplete data, which doesn't allow detection of findings under 5 mm. We are used to seeing a high-quality MRI image, yet we forget that the imaging is based on data layers, like "salami" slices, and while the imaging slices are thick, it results in low density.

Ultrasound is a powerful tool yet significantly limited by the human operator. Beyond the inherent bias of simultaneous acquisition and interpretation, the scan data is too dynamic for the human brain to process and accurately interpret in real time. The resulting subjective and incomplete information deprives patients of reliable follow-up on tumors and other health conditions, which represents the core healthcare risk.

The Human Factor

  • The accuracy of scans is operator-dependent
  • Lack of expert sonographers
  • Lack of knowledge
  • Lack of consistency in professional interpretation of medical images

Our Technology

A sophisticated approach to medical imaging, fusing robotic precision with high-density 3D ultrasound.

High-density 3D Objective Imaging

Revealan™, our robotic ultrasound imaging platform, seamlessly integrates with any off-the-shelf ultrasound device to enable operator-agnostic, automated, AI-enabled, high-density 3D imaging. The robotic scan output is a series of standardized medical images with a density at least 10 times higher than that of an MRI. With Revealan™, we allow early detection and reliable follow-ups of small findings. It stands alone as the premier solution offering a comprehensive mapping to facilitate personalized treatment.

The impact of this innovation is profound: it significantly shortens the time to diagnosis, reducing the typical years-long delay that many patients endure. By minimizing the reliance on diagnostics, it lowers procedural risks and healthcare costs. With AI-powered follow-up, our solution enables continuous monitoring, offering personalized insights and proactive management to improve long-term patient care.

New methods for the diagnosis of endometriosis rely on biomarkers, like Ziwig's Endotest, which provide a binary answer regarding the presence of endometriosis. However, these are not a comprehensive disease management tool. Therefore, we anticipate that patients will undergo such a screening test and subsequently utilize our imaging system for further evaluation and monitoring.
