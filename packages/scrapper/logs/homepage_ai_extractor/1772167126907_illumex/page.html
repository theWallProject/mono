FAQ

What is a generative semantic fabric?

illumex’s Generative Semantic Fabric (GSF) is an automated knowledge graph of semantic embeddings created from your organizational metadata and industry knowledge, combined with business logic—and with governance built-in. GSF transforms intricate data structures and siloed business logic into a format that’s easy for BI tools and large language models (LLMs) to understand.

The result? A single, reliable source of truth for all your organizational terminology and data. When deploying genAI analytics agents with GSF, you get trustworthy, context-rich, and hallucination-free responses from genAI with every interaction.

illumex’s GSF never accesses the values of your data or moves your data from its original sources. Instead, illumex automatically discovers and maps metadata from structured data sources and business application APIs, pulling it all together to create a unified knowledge graph—AKA your unique business ontology. Your ontology acts as a translator, matching users’ questions with deterministic, governed answers as it understands and interprets your natural business language along with user intent and aligns it with your organization’s specific logic and business context.

What sets illumex’s GSF apart is that it’s fully automated—unlike RAG/graphRAG or other manual solutions. This means you can get up and running with it super quickly, to get explainable, governed, and verifiable responses without being locked into specific vendors​​.

What’s more, illumex offers applications and workflows that you can deploy on top of GSF, including:

  • Full data mapping: data dictionary, documentation, lineage, data classification, ontology and taxonomy
  • Augmented AI Governance: business glossary, metric store, certification workflow, automated documentation, and classification
  • GenAI deployment SDK: definition and data retrieval questions and agentic analytics

How is illumex different from RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)?

Some of the key differences between RAG and illumex include:

1. RAG requires manual maintenance. illumex is maintained automatically.

With RAG, you’re constantly running updates and performing maintenance manually—continually refreshing your data to make sure everything is running as it should.

By contrast, illumex is powered by active metadata management, which continuously translates changes in data, and its usage, into insights for data management, governance, and analytics. This means it requires next-to-no maintenance when compared to RAG.

2. RAG doesn’t guarantee a single source of truth. illumex does.

With RAG, the question of data inconsistencies constantly hangs over your head. You’re never quite sure if you have conflicting or outdated data, or not. Resulting in the need to take extra precautions and perform additional verifications to rule these issues out.

illumex, however, provides you with a unified, consistent view of your data, automatically identifying it, classifying it, and aligning it with your business context. This ensures that when you deploy genAI agents on top of it, they’re able to interpret users’ questions and user intent with flawless precision—and match their question to the right answer, with zero hallucinations.

3. RAG needs security and governance on top of it. illumex comes with security and governance already built-in.

Because RAG interacts with sensitive data, that means it needs to be continuously monitored to make sure there are no breaches and nothing is leaked. This also means you need to have robust access controls and data sensitivity classifications in place, so you can keep tabs on all interactions with your data.

Unlike RAG, illumex comes with security and governance already built-in. From SOC 2 and GDPR to ISO 27001, illumex makes sure your sensitive data stays protected, continuously and automatically. At the same time, illumex gives you total transparency into everything it does—documenting and tracking all your data, AI, and genAI interactions so that everything is certified and explainable.

4. RAG is significantly more expensive than illumex.

Implementing RAG requires deep, specialized knowledge of LLMs, prompt engineering, and vector databases—and most companies don’t usually have this skillset already on hand. The result is that many companies end up hiring new employees or external implementation consultancies just to get RAG set up.

At the same time, with RAG, you’re hosting a vector database and making frequent API calls—which can quickly rack up costs, especially as you try to scale.

illumex, on the other hand, doesn’t require any manual setup—it’s already pretrained for you, and then it learns what it needs to automatically. At the same time, illumex supports any LLM runtime, and is designed to handle incredible volumes, and complexities, of data.
