Meet the Artisans

Sarit & Anati (Jerusalem Hills)
Two friends, Anati and Sarit, began their work in Emek Ha’Elah, a quiet valley in the Judean Hills near Bethlehem - a place layered with history and story. There, they set out to create beautiful, hand-designed games for the holiday table, pieces meant to slow people down and bring families together. As mothers and makers, they believe the table is where connection happens. Each game is thoughtfully crafted - and the intention of two friends who set out to help people gather more meaningfully.
Avi (Galilee)
In Bethlehem of the Galilee, just outside of Nazareth, is a long and windy gravel road that leads to Derech HaTavlinim, which in Hebrew means “The Spice Road”. This unique spot is now in the third generation of the Tsitershpiler family. When you enter the structure, smells of hundreds of spices waft through the air. You’ll soon see Avi, the proprietor, smiling and welcoming you toward him. For Artza, he’s put together a unique blend of spices meant to be used in the traditional Israeli breakfast dish called Shakshuka, which will bring a taste of Israel straight to your kitchen.
Tereza (Nazareth)
Just outside the center of Nazareth, on a hill overlooking the green valleys of the Galilee, is a studio where a young soap-maker named Tereza is living out her dream. A mother of three, Tereza went to school to learn how to make all natural, high quality soaps, and now sells them across Israel. Tereza created a custom, hand-made soap for Artza members that is refreshing and aromatic, and which truly highlights the best of her craft.
Oded (Jerusalem)
After playing basketball professionally in Israel, Oded Fenster decided to follow another dream: to become one of Israel’s best artisanal chocolate makers. 18 years ago, he flew to Belgium to hone his craft. Each of his chocolates, toffees and marzipans is hand-made with love, together with a staff that includes young adults with developmental challenges.     Two friends, Anati and Sarit, began their work in Emek Ha’Elah, a quiet valley in the Judean Hills near Bethlehem - a place layered with history and story. There, they set out to create beautiful, hand-designed games for the holiday table, pieces meant to slow people down and bring families together.     Avi (Galilee)
    In Bethlehem of the Galilee, just outside of Nazareth, is a long and windy gravel road that leads to Derech HaTavlinim, which in Hebrew means “The Spice Road”. This unique spot is now in the third generation of the Tsitershpiler family. When you enter the structure, smells of hundreds of spices waft through the air. You’ll soon see Avi, the proprietor, smiling and welcoming you toward him. For Artza, he’s put together a unique blend of spices meant to be used in the traditional Israeli breakfast dish called Shakshuka, which will bring a taste of Israel straight to your kitchen.

Our name, Artza

“I will bless those who bless you… and all the families of the earth shall be blessed through you…and they went out towards the Land (Artza)”
Genesis 12:3-5

Artza is Hebrew for “towards the Land.”

Inspired by Genesis 12, our name reflects a journey - toward Israel, toward faith, and toward blessing.:

A land of rolling hills and dusty paths.
A land flowing with milk and honey.
A land rich in history, faith, and promise.

Our mission is simple:
To bring you Artza - and its blessings.
