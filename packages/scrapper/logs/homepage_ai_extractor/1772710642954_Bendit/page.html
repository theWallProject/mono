News & Events

  • 01/2024

With a relatively short recovery time and minimal invasiveness, microcatheter shaping techniques are increasing in popularity in endovascular aneurysm embolization.

This review article, published recently in Frontiers in Neurology, discusses the increased popularity of the endovascular intervention technique for the treatment of intracranial aneurysms. iSpanning a period of 30 years, the article highlights the importance of this technique and the challenges faced by neurointerventionalists using microcatheters for this procedure.

By allowing for both shaping and navigation of the microcatheter, Bendit, the steerable guidewire and microcatheter presents a new paradigm for treatment, benefiting both clinician and patient.

For an in-depth look at the various methods developed over the past decade see the full article >> Navigating complexity: a comprehensive review of microcatheter shaping techniques

  • 12/2023

The Bendit team is excited to share a peer-reviewed analysis of a human trial of Bendit 21!
The primary endpoints of this exploratory, prospective, multicenter, open-label, single-arm clinical study were device related safety events. These included the successful navigation of through the ‘challenging vessel anatomy’ and successful treatment delivery.
For a complete documentation of all 25 patients who were treated see the full journal study >> Clinical experience with the Bendit steerable microcatheter

  • 11/2023

Due to complex vascular anatomy and unstable devices, intracranial vascular aneurysms can be a challenge to treat. While conventional microcatheters have a fixed, non-flexible shape, the Bendit steerable microcatheter maintains torsional rigidity combined with real-time bending and torquing capabilities that can be used without a guidewire.

Check out this article published in the Journal of NeuroInterventional Surgery to see how researchers used the Bendit microcatheters to successfully treat side-wall aneurysms in animal models
