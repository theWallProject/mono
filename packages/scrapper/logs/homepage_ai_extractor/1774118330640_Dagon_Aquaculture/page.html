    The Maagan Michael Bird Park is a place where the coastline truly comes to life. What you see today is the result of a local connection with Dagon Aquaculture.

    A portion of the ponds that now provide a home for local plants and wildlife were previously used for fish farming. By working together, these areas were made available to the park, allowing the natural habitat to recover and thrive.
    This is a simple example of how aquaculture and conservation can coexist, ensuring these spaces serve a meaningful purpose for the environment.

    Working together for nature, The Society for the Protection of Nature in Israel and Dagon Aquaculture.

    We are #microalgae lovers too.
    Ever wondered how #TetraselmisSuecica supports aquaculture?

    A single microalga can play an important role from the very beginning.
    Tetraselmis suecica is a motile green microalga, moving through the water with four flagella. Its mobility and biological properties make it especially useful in larval rearing systems.
    It helps green larval tanks, stabilizing the environment and supporting early larval development. It also serves as an important nutritional source for rotifers during their first feeding stages.
    In addition, #Tetraselmis produces natural bioactive compounds that help support healthier larvae and fingerlings.

    Our Markets

    ​

    Being a multi-species hatchery, we are producing all our fingerling types year-round, in response to our markets' demand. 

    Making the most out of our local Israeli market, we are now focused on marketing Seabass, Seabream and Grey Mullet to fish farms in Europe, North Africa, and the Middle East. The demand for both our Barramundi and Hybrid Striped Bass has grown dramatically in the US and Europe, and most of our Red Drum products are demanded by our local markets for ecological solutions.
